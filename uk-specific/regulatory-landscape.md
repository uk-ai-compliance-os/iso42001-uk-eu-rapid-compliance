# UK Regulatory Landscape for AI Compliance
## How UK Law Intersects with ISO 42001 and the EU AI Act

### 1. The UK AI White Paper (March 2023)
Five principles for AI regulation:
1. **Safety, security and robustness**
2. **Appropriate transparency and explainability**
3. **Fairness**
4. **Accountability and governance**
5. **Contestability and redress**

ISO 42001 directly operationalizes principles 1, 2, 4, and 5.

### 2. ICO AI and Data Protection Risk Toolkit
The Information Commissioner's Office expects:
- Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA) for AI processing personal data
- Transparency about automated decision-making (UK GDPR Article 22)
- Fairness testing across demographic groups
- Human review of solely automated decisions with legal/significant effects

ISO 42001 Clause 8.2 (AI system impact assessment) and Clause 8.5 (deployment) satisfy ICO expectations when aligned with DPIA methodology.

### 3. FCA Expectations (Financial Services)
- FG 23/3: AI systems must be transparent, explainable, and fair.
- Senior Managers & Certification Regime (SMCR): Individual accountability for AI outcomes.
- Operational resilience: AI systems must meet same standards as other critical services.

### 4. MHRA (Healthtech)
- Software as Medical Device (SaMD) guidance.
- AIaMD (AI as Medical Device) specific requirements.
- Post-market surveillance mandatory.

### 5. The Critical Point: EU AI Act Extraterritoriality
UK companies are **not exempt**. If your AI system:
- Is placed on the EU market
- Is used in the EU
- Affects EU citizens

You are a **provider** or **deployer** under the EU AI Act and must comply with:
- High-risk system requirements (Annex III)
- Conformity assessment
- CE marking / UKCA alignment strategy
- Post-market monitoring

**Deadline: August 2, 2026**
